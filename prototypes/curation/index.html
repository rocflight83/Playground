Building an automated options trading system

Target: Run an unattended paper-trading options system with one backtested, regime-gated strategy whose alpha thesis, favourable regime and failure modes you can state in writing; a written rubric you have used to score and rank three sourced strategies; and a repeatable agentic workflow that adds a second strategy behind the same backtest and risk gates

How this plan was built (DISSS)

Deconstruction

The subject breaks into nine minimal effective units. (1) variance risk premium: why selling options has historically paid and who is paying for it. (2) greeks & pricing: Black-Scholes as a translation layer between an option position and its exposures to price, time and volatility. (3) regime identification: VIX level, VIX term structure and trend as observable state variables that decide when a strategy is on or off. (4) backtest & overfitting discipline: walk-forward testing, parameter sensitivity and the honest accounting that keeps a backtest from lying. (5) strategy evaluation rubric: the fixed set of questions you ask any sourced strategy before it gets code. (6) strategy interface & risk gates: the one code contract every strategy implements and the gates that reject a trade. (7) broker/data adapter: the thin layer that talks to option chains, orders and historical series. (8) agentic build loop: spec, tests, implement, review, done with a coding agent and encoded in the repo's memory files. (9) position sizing: Kelly-style bet sizing and portfolio-level exposure limits.

Selection rationale

Roughly 80% of the target sits in four units: variance risk premium, regime identification, backtest & overfitting discipline and the agentic build loop. The first two are what 'understand the alpha and its regime' actually means for options; the third is the only thing that separates a sourced strategy from a story; the fourth is how the system keeps growing after session 14. Greeks are included only as a risk language, not as a pricing research topic. The strategy interface, broker adapter and position sizing are included because the system does not run without them, but each gets exactly the depth the artifact needs.

Cut list

Dropped for speed: stochastic and local volatility models; volatility surface fitting and smile dynamics; market-making and order-book microstructure; intraday or high-frequency execution; machine-learning signal research; multi-leg exotic structures (dispersion, variance swaps, ratio structures); tax and margin optimisation; a full historical option-chain database (the backtest uses synthetic pricing from index and VIX series plus Cboe benchmark indices; a paid data feed is an upgrade path, not a requirement); live-money deployment.

Sequencing rationale

The plan runs backwards from the endgame. Session 1 builds the real thing in miniature: a strategy interface, a broker adapter and one end-to-end paper order, so every later session fills in a working skeleton rather than preparing for one. The alpha thesis (session 2) and greeks (session 3) come next because the week-one backtest needs them; the regime layer and the rubric arrive once there is a strategy to gate and something to compare against. Forward sequencing was rejected: nothing here requires finishing pricing theory before placing a paper order, and starting with theory would delay the first working system to week two.

01The endgame in miniature
Sessions 1–3

Outlier Story: Edward O. Thorp

Unusual Approach: A mathematics professor who had already beaten blackjack with card counting, Thorp treated the options and warrant market as another casino: he derived a pricing formula and a delta-hedging method before Black-Scholes was published, then ran a hedge fund on it for two decades without a losing year.

Transferable Principle: Quantify the edge and the bet size before placing the trade; a strategy is a bet with odds you can state, not a story about the market.

Citation: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_O._Thorp

02Backtest honestly
Sessions 4–7

Outlier Story: Ray Dalio and Bridgewater's All Weather portfolio

Unusual Approach: Rather than trying to predict which market regime came next, Dalio's team asked which economic environments exist (growth and inflation rising or falling), then balanced exposures so that in each environment some part of the portfolio would pay.

Transferable Principle: 'Profitable in any regime' is a property of a combination of strategies that win in different regimes, never of one strategy that wins in all of them.

Citation: https://www.bridgewater.com/research-and-insights/the-all-weather-story

03Judge strategies
Sessions 8–10

Outlier Story: Jim Simons

Unusual Approach: A geometer and Cold War codebreaker who hired mathematicians, physicists and signal-processing scientists instead of finance professionals, Simons built Renaissance's Medallion fund on statistically tested rules with no discretionary overrides.

Transferable Principle: Systematic, tested rules beat narratives; the system decides the trade, and the operator's job is to improve the system.

Citation: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Simons
